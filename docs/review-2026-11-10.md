# Concept and hardware review (2026-11-10)

Status: 2026-11-10 · Review of the current concept, the main board, the charger board, the mechanics, the firmware
plan and the operation concept. Read: all documents in `docs/`, `hardware/bom/parts.csv`, the schematic generator,
`sleeve.scad`, `board_outline.py`, `routing.json`, and the step-0 firmware in `firmware/louie-fw`. No hardware was
measured and nothing was built; everything below is a desk review against the documents and the design data.

Open items that already exist (F/A/T/S/L/P numbers) are referenced, not repeated. New findings carry the prefix
**R**. Decisions stay with the project owner; where this review makes a recommendation it says so.

## Rating

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Important** | Fix or decide before the prototype order, or before firmware step 4 (motor). Otherwise a board revision, a rework or a concept change later is likely. |
| **Good** | Worth doing in this revision: small effort, clear gain, low risk. |
| **Optional** | Later revision, or only if a test shows the need. |
| *(verify)* | A measurement or a look at the real toy decides; the proposal says what to measure. |

## Summary

The concept is thorough and the hard questions (motor driver, lever detection, power path, coexistence, protocol,
backpressure, OTA) are analysed to a depth that is unusual for a hobby project. The datasheet review found no wiring
errors. The main risks are now not analysis gaps but **things only a physical test can answer** and a handful of
**interactions between subsystems** that the single-topic documents do not cover:

1. **Brake masks the lever** (R-C1): in brake and in every "motor off" phase, SENSE reads "lever open" and no
   current flows, so neither detection method works. Every motor-off state must be coast, and the step-0 firmware
   does the opposite.
2. **4 × AA support degraded the primary Li-ion case** (R-B1): higher LDO dropout and quiescent current, a thermal
   limit of the LDO in hotspot mode, the Q1 body-diode case, and the charger board is incompatible. This deserves an
   explicit trade-off decision.
3. **Nobody has looked inside the toys yet** (R-E1): the LiPo in the base, the cable past the lid and the upright
   charger board all assume a passage between battery compartment and base cavity that is unverified for both models.
4. **The status LED is invisible in normal use** (R-A4): it sits in a closed battery compartment, but several
   concepts (network mode, gesture windows, Zigbee mode) treat it as the only display.
5. **The project loop has run without hardware feedback for a long time** (R-A1): the dev board costs 14 €, the
   prototype batch about 105 €, and most remaining open items are measurements.

Recommended order: R-A3/R-E1 (open the toys, 30 min) → R-B1 decision → R-C1 rule into the firmware concept → trivial
schematic fixes (R-B3, R-D1, R-D2) → finish routing by hand (R-A2) → order → dev board and pre-order checks in
parallel.

## B. Power supply and power path

| # | Finding | Why it matters | Proposal | Rating |

## C. Motor, lever detection and safety

| # | Finding | Why it matters | Proposal | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R-C1** | **Brake masks the lever.** In brake (IN1 = IN2 = 1) both low-side FETs are on, OUT_A and OUT_B sit at GND, so SENSE reads 0 V = "lever open", and no motor current flows, so the current-based detection (hardware-concept.md 5.2) is blind as well. The same holds for every "motor off" phase that uses brake: `pause` segments, the "air pocket" spice, the post-stall brake, and setpoint 0 in main.rs (`State::Run` with `output_mv == 0` → `motor.brake()`). On the real board main.rs would then read SENSE low → `LeverOpen` → coast → SENSE high → `Run` → brake … at the tick rate. In addition, while driving forward SENSE is a 20 kHz PWM between 0 and about 3.3 V, and in reverse it is permanently LOW; the digital read `hw::level(p.sense)` in the Run state is therefore random. The concept knows "SENSE is not meaningful while driving", but no document states the consequence for brake and motor-off phases, and the step-0 code does not implement the current-based path. | This is the one interaction that would make the prototype look broken on day one, and it decides how `pause`, `stall` and stop are implemented in the core. | Rules for firmware-architecture.md 4.1: **every motor-off state is coast** (0/0 → autosleep, SENSE valid after about 1 ms); brake only as a transient (≤ a few 100 ms, e.g. the hard drop of a sawtooth, the stall reaction), with SENSE ignored during it; after a stall → brake 200 ms → coast. **While driving, the lever is evaluated by current only** (R-C2). Fix main.rs before it ever runs on `board-louie-v1` (coast at setpoint 0; lever from SENSE only when the driver is in coast). Document in main-board-c6.md 2.5. | **Important** |
| **R-C2** | **"Lever open while driving" by current alone is weak at low duty.** At minimum speed the motor current may be 100–200 mA → 25–50 mV at the ADC, against noise and offset; A6 covers accuracy, not the decision rule. | A false "lever open" stops the round; a missed one keeps PWM running into an open circuit (harmless, but the round state is wrong). | Define the rule: current < X mA for > 100 ms while duty > Y → suspect; confirm with a **short coast window** (2–5 ms every 100 ms) in which SENSE is sampled. The window costs about 2–5 % torque and nothing audible; the DRV8213 wakes from autosleep in 250 µs. This makes lever detection independent of the current scale. | **Good** |
| **R-C3** | **Lever semantics are not defined per state.** "Lever on = start" (software-concept.md 5.1) conflicts with: the lever gesture "next mode" in READY (firmware-architecture.md 6.4), the 3× handover gesture (web-ui-concept.md 4.1), the wizard's "confirm with the lever", pairing and test operation. The web UI concept solves some of these with explicit windows; the offline "next mode" gesture has no window. | Without one table, each feature will interpret the lever differently. | One table "lever edge → effect" per state (SLEEP, boot, READY, COUNTDOWN, RUNNING, PAUSE, END, wizard, gesture window, workshop). Use the existing **countdown** (web-ui-concept.md 5.1) as the gesture window: lever close in READY → countdown 1–3 s → start; further flips during the countdown are gestures. Countdown length as owner setting, 0 allowed when gestures are off. | **Good** |
| **R-C4** | **Wake-by-lever then needs a second flip (S1 open).** The lever closing *is* the wake event; "lever on at wake-up → no round starts" means the phone-less user (scenario S8) flips, nothing happens for 2–5 s, flips again. The surprise-start concern (lever bumped while the toy is put away) exists with the original toy as well, where the lever is the power switch. | The "playable without a phone" promise hinges on this. | Recommendation for S1: after a wake by SENSE, start the lever mode if the lever is **still closed about 1 s after boot** (motor thread before Wi-Fi, R-G8); keep "start only after release" as the owner option. | **Good** (decision) |
| **R-C5** | **Destructive lever gestures.** "Flip 10× at start resets the admin password and deletes the Wi-Fi networks" (software-concept.md 4.2). A child playing with the lever while the toy is switched on can wipe the configuration. S3 asks about reliability, not about the consequence. | Data loss by an unintentional gesture is worse than a slightly longer recovery path. | Gestures never delete anything. A gesture **opens a 10-minute open recovery hotspot** with a "reset owner password" button and shows the hotspot credentials; Wi-Fi networks stay. Ties to R-H1. | **Good** |
| **R-C6** | **IO2 floats when U4 is not fitted and J2 is empty.** `sleep.rs` disables the internal pulls on all `wake.high` pins, and INT has no external pull (R10/R16 are on SDA/SCL). A floating LP GPIO as an ext1 wake source gives random wake-ups and standby drain. | Pure firmware rule, easy to forget. | Enable the IO2 wake only when the I²C probe found the sensor or the owner enabled an external switch; otherwise LP pull-down on IO2. Note in firmware-architecture.md 7 (power). | **Good** |
| **R-C7** | **PWM at 20 kHz is at the edge of children's hearing.** The target audience hears up to about 18–20 kHz; motor and board may whine at the PWM fundamental. | Audible whine on a children's toy is a complaint; the fix is free. | 24–25 kHz (LEDC at 80 MHz still gives ≥ 11 bit). Test with children on the prototype (T9). | **Optional** |
| **R-C8** | **SENSE has no capacitor.** IO3 hangs on a 470 k source impedance connected to 30 cm of motor wiring through the toy; ESD and brush noise reach the pin directly (there is no TVS on OUT_A/OUT_B either, rejected for space). | Levels are fine; robustness is the question. Only a prototype test can tell. | A DNP 0402 footprint 1–10 nF IO3 → GND if routing allows (τ ≤ 5 ms with 470 k, no effect on the lever levels or the wake). Otherwise accept and do the ESD check in test-hardware.md 6. | **Optional** |
| **R-C9** | **F17 (IN1/IN2 during reset and deep sleep).** The analysis is complete; the measurement in 4.4 decides. | – | Keep the plan. Note that with R-C1 the firmware anyway must keep the driver in coast whenever it is not driving, so option a) (no pull-downs) only needs deep-sleep hold to work. | **Optional** *(verify)* |

## Recommended order

1. **Open both toys** (R-A3, R-E1, R-D7): passage to the base, cavity, compartment width at the ribs, lid, diode in series. Half an hour, decides the mechanics.
2. **Decide R-B1** (4 × AA vs. Li-ion-first) and write the supply table (R-I1, R-B2).
3. **Concept rules into the documents:** R-C1 (coast is the only motor-off state), R-C3 (lever table), R-C4/S1, R-C5, R-H1, R-G4.
4. **Schematic freeze with the cheap fixes:** R-B3, R-D1, R-D2, R-D3 (pad shape after the print test), optionally R-C8.
5. **Finish routing by hand** (R-A2), DRC 0, JLC preview, order 5 boards with H8.
6. **In parallel:** dev board, `just fw-build` (R-G1), pre-order checks 4.1–4.5, print tests (R-E3), fix main.rs (R-G2).
7. Charger board v0.2 list (R-F2, R-F1) for the second order.
